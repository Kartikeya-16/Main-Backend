# Financial Advisor datasets

Reference data and test profiles for the Arthsaathi Financial Advisor (`advisor-service`).
Everything here is loaded into memory at startup; no database is needed.

## Files

| File | What it holds | Refresh |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `reference/fd_rates.csv` | 28 banks (7 PSU, 11 private, 10 small finance) × best slab, 1y, 3y, 5y, plus 5-year tax-saver where offered. General and senior rates, deposits below Rs 3 crore. 135 rows. | Monthly, and after every RBI rate decision |
| `reference/small_savings.csv` | 12 government small savings schemes with rate, lock-in, limits, tax treatment and eligibility, for Oct–Dec 2026. | Every quarter (new rates are notified around the last day of each quarter) |
| `reference/instrument_categories.csv` | 16 investment categories (not named funds) with horizon, risk level, liquidity and planning return assumptions. FD and small-savings rows point to the two files above for their real rates. | Yearly review |
| `reference/rules_config.yaml` | Every threshold the engine uses: emergency fund months, debt cutoff, insurance rules, action order, risk questionnaire and bands, equity share per bucket, planning returns, DICGC and tax rules. | When you change a rule |
| `fixtures/personas.json` | 20 user profiles with the advice the engine must produce for each. Golden tests for pytest. | Regenerate only when a rule changes |

## Sources and dates

- **Bank FD rates:** Paisabazaar comparison tables, snapshot of 14 October 2026 (general and senior pages). This is an aggregator, so confirm a bank's rate on its own site before showing it as final. The RBI raised the repo rate to 5.50% on 7 October 2026 and banks are still revising, so expect changes.
- **Small savings rates:** Ministry of Finance office memorandum dated 30 September 2026 (Department of Posts SB Order 12/2026), rates unchanged for 1 October–31 December 2026. Scheme-wise table via TaxGuru.
- **DICGC cover:** Rs 5 lakh per depositor per bank, principal plus interest (dicgc.org.in).
- **Tax deduction:** the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026. Old Section 80C is now Section 123 read with Schedule XV, still capped at Rs 1.5 lakh and still available only under the old tax regime. The CSVs use the neutral column `tax_deduction_eligible`.
- **Scheme rules** (lock-ins, limits, eligibility) are standard post-office rules; the `notes` column flags the ones with exceptions.

Return figures in `instrument_categories.csv` and `planning_assumptions` in the YAML are conservative planning assumptions, not forecasts. Say so in the viva and in the UI.

## How the persona tests work

`tools/validate_datasets.py` contains a small reference implementation of the rules in `rules_config.yaml`. It:

1. checks every CSV for missing columns, duplicates, out-of-range rates, senior rate below general rate, tax-saver lock-ins and stale dates (FD data older than 45 days, or a small-savings quarter that has ended);
2. checks the risk bands cover scores 5–20 exactly once;
3. runs every persona through the reference rules and compares the result with its `expected` block.

```bash
cd advisor-service
python tools/validate_datasets.py           # should print OK
python tools/validate_datasets.py --regen   # only after an intentional rule change; review the diff
```

The real advisor engine should import `load_all()` and the personas from this script in pytest and assert it produces the same `expected` output.

The `expected` blocks were generated by the reference rules and reviewed by hand (spot check: p19's car goal, Rs 6 lakh in 2 years at 0% equity, needs Rs 26,380 a month after 6% inflation; worked out manually to the same figure).

## What the personas cover

Budget deficit, critical and partial emergency fund gaps, high-interest debt (and debt that should not be flagged, such as an 8.75% home loan or a 10.5% education loan), missing health and term cover, the no-income and age-60 risk caps, short goals forcing 0% equity even for aggressive investors, SCSS and SSY eligibility, old vs new tax regime, and 7 profiles whose goals cost more than their monthly surplus.
